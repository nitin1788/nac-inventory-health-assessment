Before a nearby customer ever sees your website, sees an ad, or walks through your door, most of them see your Google Business Profile first — your hours, your location on the map, your reviews, a few photos. It's free, it's the single highest-leverage thing most pharmacies and medical stores under-invest in, and most of what makes it work is genuinely just completing it properly and keeping it current. This is the checklist we use as a starting point on our own [Google Business Profile](/services/digital-marketing/google-business-profile) engagements.

## 1. Get the basics exactly right

- **Category:** Choose "Pharmacy" or the closest accurate match — not a generic "store" category. This affects which searches you're eligible to show up for at all.
- **Name, address, phone:** Must match your website and every other listing exactly (see [our local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-pharmacies-medical-stores-guide) for why consistency matters this much).
- **Hours:** Keep these current, including holidays and any temporary changes — an outdated hours listing sends a customer away frustrated, not just uninformed.
- **Website link:** Point to your actual site, not a generic landing page unrelated to your store.

## 2. Fill in every attribute that applies

Attributes like "delivery available," "wheelchair accessible," or "open 24 hours" aren't decorative — they're what let Google match your profile to a more specific search, like "24-hour pharmacy near me" or "pharmacy with delivery near me." Leaving these blank means you're invisible to exactly the searches where you'd otherwise be a strong match.

## 3. Add real photos, and keep adding them

A profile with a handful of real, current photos — your storefront, your interior, your team — reads as an active, trustworthy business. A profile with no photos, or photos that are years old, reads the opposite way, even if nothing else has changed. This isn't a one-time task; treat it as something you add to periodically, not something you finish once.

## 4. Ask for reviews, and respond to every one

Reviews do two jobs at once: they build trust with a customer deciding whether to visit, and they factor directly into how Google ranks your listing locally. Make asking for a review part of a good interaction — after a smooth prescription pickup, after resolving a customer's question — rather than leaving it to chance. And respond to every review you get, positive or negative; an unanswered negative review sitting there unaddressed is worse than the review itself.

## 5. Post updates, don't just set it and forget it

Google Business Profile lets you publish short updates directly on your listing — a new service, a seasonal reminder, a stock update. Profiles that post regularly read as active; profiles that haven't been touched in months read as possibly closed or unreliable, even when they're not.

## 6. Why all of this actually affects whether you're found

Google ranks local results on three things: **relevance** (how well your profile matches what someone searched for), **distance** (how close you are to the searcher), and **prominence** — Google's own materials also call this **popularity** — meaning your overall reputation: reviews, citations, and how complete and active your profile is. You can't do anything about distance, but relevance and prominence are almost entirely under your control, and they're exactly what items 1 through 5 above are building. A profile that's accurate, complete, photographed, reviewed, and actively updated is doing everything it can on the two factors that are actually yours to influence.

## The checklist

- [ ] Correct category (Pharmacy / Medical Store, not generic)
- [ ] Name, address, phone identical to your website and other listings
- [ ] Hours current, including holidays
- [ ] Every relevant attribute filled in (delivery, accessibility, hours-related tags)
- [ ] At least a handful of real, current photos
- [ ] A simple habit for asking satisfied customers for reviews
- [ ] Every review — good or bad — has a response
- [ ] A recent post published on the profile
