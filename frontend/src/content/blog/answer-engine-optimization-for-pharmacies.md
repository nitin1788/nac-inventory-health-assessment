Increasingly, "is there a pharmacy near me that's open now" doesn't lead to a page of search results at all — it leads to one answer, generated by Google's AI Overview or a tool like ChatGPT, naming one or two businesses. If you're not one of the sources that answer draws from, good traditional SEO doesn't automatically save you. This is what Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) actually is, and what it takes for a pharmacy specifically.

## 1. This shift is bigger than it might seem

One industry estimate puts nearly half of healthcare-related, first-page Google searches as now triggering an AI-generated overview rather than a plain list of links, alongside a reported drop in organic click-through rates for healthcare searches generally — with the visitors who do still click through described as considerably more valuable than a typical organic visitor. (These are one agency's own published figures, not independently verified here — but the direction, more searches getting answered by AI before a user ever sees a ranked list, matches what's visibly happening across search generally.) Whatever the exact numbers turn out to be for any given pharmacy, the underlying shift is real enough to plan around now.

## 2. AEO doesn't replace local SEO — it depends on it

This is the part most breathless "AI search is changing everything" content skips: AI tools synthesize their answer from the sources they already trust most — your Google Business Profile, your reviews and ratings, clear question-and-answer content on your own site, and consistent name/address/phone citations across the web. That's precisely the same foundation covered in [our local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-pharmacies-medical-stores-guide) and [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist-for-pharmacies) — AEO is an extension of those fundamentals, not a separate project. A pharmacy with an incomplete profile and no reviews isn't going to start getting cited in AI answers just by doing something new; it needs the same groundwork first.

## 3. A few things specific to how AI tools pick a source

Beyond the local SEO basics, a few things matter more for AI visibility specifically:

- **Review quality, not just review count** — ratings meaningfully above average, with an active pattern of responses, read as more trustworthy to a system trying to pick a safe recommendation.
- **Consistency across your whole online footprint**, not just your own website — directories, Bing Places, and other listings all feed into how confidently an AI system can verify who you are.
- **Clear, direct answers to real questions** on your own site (do you offer home delivery, are you open on Sundays, do you stock a certain category) — content written to actually answer a specific question tends to be exactly what gets quoted back.

## 4. The technical layer underneath: structured data

Behind the scenes, structured data (schema markup) is what tells search engines and AI tools, in a standardized format, exactly what your business is — its name, address, hours, and category — rather than making them guess from unstructured text. One industry report found businesses with properly implemented schema markup got cited in AI responses several times more often than those without it. This is a website-level technical detail rather than something you write content about, but it's worth confirming is actually in place, not assumed.

## 5. What we actually did for our own site

Rather than just describing this in the abstract, our own site publishes a plain-text `llms.txt` file — a concise, structured summary of who we are, what we do, and who we serve, written specifically for AI systems to read and cite accurately, separate from the version of our site built for human visitors. It's a small, concrete example of applying AEO fundamentals rather than just writing about them, and it's the kind of thing we set up as part of a properly built [SEO](/services/digital-marketing/seo) foundation.
