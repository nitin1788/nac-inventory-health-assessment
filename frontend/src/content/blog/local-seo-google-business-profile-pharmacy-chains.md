Everything in [our local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-pharmacies-medical-stores-guide) and [Google Business Profile checklist](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist-for-pharmacies) is written for a single store. Once a pharmacy has two, five, or twenty branches, that playbook doesn't just need scaling up — it needs a different structure, because each location is actually competing for its own "near me" search, separately from every other branch.

## 1. This is a real, sizeable segment, not a niche case

Regional multi-outlet pharmacy chains are a genuine part of the market — chains like Wellness Forever (well over 100 stores in Mumbai alone) and Noble Chemist (reportedly grown to dozens of outlets across multiple states) are established, real examples of pharmacy businesses that have grown well beyond a single location. If your chain is anywhere on that path, the single-location approach starts working against you rather than for you.

## 2. Every location needs its own Google Business Profile

A single profile trying to represent five branches can only show one address, one set of hours, and one review history — which means it can only ever properly serve the searches happening near one of those locations. Each branch needs its own claimed, fully completed profile, with its own accurate hours, photos, and reviews specific to that store. This is the multi-location version of the same completeness work covered in our GBP checklist, just repeated per branch rather than done once.

## 3. Every location needs its own page, not a shared locations list

A single "our locations" page listing five addresses in a row doesn't give Google — or a customer — anything specific to match against a local search. A dedicated page per branch, with that branch's actual address, hours, and any location-specific details, gives each one a real chance to rank for its own nearby searches. Structure these as subfolders on your main site (`/locations/andheri/`, `/locations/thane/`) rather than separate subdomains, so all the SEO value stays consolidated under one domain instead of being split across several.

## 4. NAP consistency now applies per location, not once

The name/address/phone consistency covered in the single-location guide still applies — but now it has to hold true separately for every branch, across your website, each location's Google Business Profile, and every directory listing. A phone number that's correct for four branches and wrong for the fifth undermines that one location specifically, even if everything else is in order.

## 5. Consolidated reporting matters as much as consolidated marketing

The same instinct that makes chain owners want one dashboard for stock across all their stores — covered in [Chain Pharmacy](/industries/chain-pharmacy) and [KPI/MIS/Dashboards](/services/inventory-operations-consulting/kpi-mis-dashboards) — applies to marketing performance too. Knowing which branch's local search visibility is actually working, and which one needs attention, requires looking at [Analytics & Conversion Tracking](/services/digital-marketing/analytics-conversion-tracking) broken out by location, not lumped into one number for the whole chain.

## 6. Plan for verification to take longer

Claiming and verifying a Google Business Profile for a brand-new location is often a manual process on Google's side, and it can take time — plan the rollout of a new branch's local presence as its own small project, not something that happens automatically the day the store opens.
